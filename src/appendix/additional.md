## Scientific Tributes

### [80th Birthday Article](80th-birthday.md)

A tribute written by colleagues Joseph A. Rard and John G. Albright celebrating Don Miller's 80th birthday and his outstanding contributions to physical chemistry and the thermodynamics of linear irreversible processes. ## Musical Compositions

Donald Miller was not only a distinguished scientist and civic leader, but also an accomplished composer of ragtime piano music. Below are three original compositions that showcase his musical talents and his connections to his community.

### [Nostalgia Rag](nrag.md)

An original ragtime piano composition written in March tempo. This piece captures the nostalgic spirit of early American popular music with characteristic syncopated rhythms and lively melodies.

**Copyright:** ©1971, 1991 by Donald G. Miller, Livermore, CA

[Download Sheet Music (PDF)](nrag.pdf)

### [Political Rag](prag.md)

A ragtime composition dedicated to Joan Seppala and the staff of the Livermore Independent newspaper. This piece reflects Don's deep involvement in local politics and journalism, combining his passion for music with his commitment to civic engagement.

**Dedication:** Joan Seppala and the staff of the Independent Newspaper, Livermore, CA

**Copyright:** ©1975, 1994 by Donald G. Miller, Livermore, CA

[Download Sheet Music (PDF)](prag.pdf)

### [Centennial Rag](crag.md)

A celebratory ragtime piece composed for Livermore's 100th anniversary in 1969. Dedicated to all citizens of Livermore, this composition demonstrates Don's deep connection to his community and his ability to express civic pride through music.

**Dedication:** The Citizens of Livermore, California 1869-1969

**Copyright:** © 1969 by Donald G. Miller, Livermore, California

[Download Sheet Music (PDF)](crag.pdf)

## About Don's Musical Interests

Don Miller's love for ragtime and classical music was well-known among family and friends. His compositions demonstrate his technical skill as a pianist and his understanding of the ragtime genre, which flourished in America from the 1890s through the 1910s. These pieces represent the creative side of a man whose talents extended far beyond the laboratory and city hall.
